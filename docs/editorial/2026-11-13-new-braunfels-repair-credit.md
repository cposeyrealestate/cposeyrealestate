### Verified primary factual sources, checked November13,2026

1. CFPB, Schedule a home inspection: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/close/schedule-a-home-inspection/
   Supports negotiating repairs or a credit; major property repairs can complicate closing. A lender may require repairs before closing or an approved repair account. It does not establish a universal right to a credit, automatic cancellation right, or automatic eligibility for a holdback.
2. TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale), Form20-19, PDF dated05-04-2026: https://www.trec.texas.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-forms/20-19.pdf
   Paragraph7D: as-is acceptance does not remove inspection access, subsequent repair negotiations or an option-period termination right where applicable. 7E handles lender-required repairs and payment agreement. 7F generally requires agreed repairs before closing unless otherwise agreed in writing, applicable permits, appropriate repair professionals, scope/payment documentation and transfer of transferable warranties. Explain the signed contract rather than asserting all sales use this form.
3. TREC Seller's Disclosure Notice55-1, page effective05/28/2026: https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/sellers-disclosure-notice
   Physical condition/material-fact disclosure under Texas Property Code5.008. Avoid saying every sale has identical duties; statutory exceptions exist. Do not say a credit or as-is marketing removes applicable disclosure obligations.
4. Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-4.1-02: https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.1-02/interested-party-contributions-ipcs
   Financing concessions pay eligible borrower closing costs/prepaids and must not exceed actual closing costs; occupancy and loan-to-value affect applicable limits. Excess amounts have sales-concession treatment. A negotiated dollar credit is not necessarily unrestricted cash for the buyer. Do not apply Fannie Mae percentages to all loans or repeat a blanket VA4percent cap.
5. Texas Real Estate Research Center, Navigating As Is Sales: https://trerc.tamu.edu/article/Navigating-As-Is-Sale-2381/
   Background explanation of disclosure and as-is acceptance, published2023. Current TREC form remains controlling factual reference for this brief.
6. Zillow Research, Sellers: Results from Consumer Housing Trends Report2026, October14,2026: https://www.zillow.com/research/sellers-consumer-housing-trends-report-2026/
   National survey context only. No inference of local search demand, local repair costs or guaranteed resale return. Avoid unnecessary statistics when the decision can be explained without them.

### Prepared editorial framework, pending demand gate

Opening answer: Compare a written repair quote and completion date with the credit the buyer can actually use. Diagnose safety, structural or active-water problems before choosing a concession. For a documented cosmetic issue, a credit may preserve buyer choice, but confirm lender rules, disclose applicable conditions and put the final agreement in writing.

Early comparison table columns: issue / repair route / credit route / what to verify. Rows: active defect needing diagnosis; lender-required condition; cosmetic preference; small documented functional repair; repair that delays listing or closing. Avoid automatic must-fix rules based solely on age, a crack photograph or a generic inspector checklist.

Body plan:
1. Identify the actual defect and get an appropriate professional assessment; distinguish repair from upgrading a working item.
2. Compare written scope, exclusions, quote expiration, completion date, permits where applicable and warranty documentation. Do not invent New Braunfels average contractor costs.
3. Compare cash and timing using an explicitly hypothetical example: a6000dollar repair plus1000dollar incremental delay cost versus an8000dollar lender-approved credit, all other terms equal. Repair path costs7000versus8000, a1000difference, before any unknown price effect or overrun. This is arithmetic, not local pricing or promised ROI. If the defect prevents financing, the credit path may be unavailable.
4. Confirm eligible credit use and existing concessions with lender/title before agreeing. A price reduction, closing-cost credit and cash repair payment have different effects; do not treat them as interchangeable.
5. Document disclosure, signed repair/credit terms, deadlines and completion evidence. Link the buyer inspection guide for the other side of negotiations.
6. Next step: contact Cody with the actual condition, written quotes and move timeline for a property-specific comparison; /contact/ and /sellers-net-sheet/ only after confirming actual internal route names before implementation.

Four proposed FAQ answers grounded in the verified sources:
- Can I offer a credit instead of fixing a lender-required repair? Do not assume so. The lender must approve the property's condition and any permitted treatment; a credit alone does not repair a defect.
- Does a repair credit give the buyer cash after closing? Not necessarily. A closing-cost credit is subject to the loan's eligible uses and limits. Confirm treatment with lender/title before making an offer.
- Does selling as-is mean I can skip disclosure? No general exemption follows from as-is wording. Applicable statutory disclosures and the signed contract still matter.
- Should I replace an old system that still works? Age alone does not answer the decision. Assess actual condition, a documented quote, lender requirements where relevant, and the cost/timing tradeoff. Do not promise replacement will recover its cost.
